\paragraph{Grounded in the testbed.} Assumptions that the simplified settings left to sensitivity estimates are built into the grounded condition, so their effects appear directly in Table~\ref{tab:grounded}: page weight matched to real pages; measured page and post latency, with an assumed 0.3\,s per API call; published human response times; documents only after the Loan Estimate, which Reg~Z allows, with the estimate arriving between two sessions in place of an artificial processing delay; and market loan figures.

\paragraph{Sensitivity.}
The ranking of the set-ups survives every remaining change we tested; the size of the gaps depends most on how long people take. Where the logs allow, we quantified each change instead of arguing it (scripts in the artifact).
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Human response time.} We use the study's 16.6\,s median SMS time for every code, consent and approval, and assume 60\,s per answer. If every human step took two minutes, grounded wall time would be about 1{,}410\,s for websites, 729\,s for C1 and 625\,s for C2, and the website-to-code ratio would fall from 4.8$\times$ to 2.3$\times$. With standing grants C1 would take 254\,s and C2 154\,s.
\item \textbf{Latency.} At 0.1 or 0.67\,s per API call, C1 would take 250 or 265\,s and C2 147 or 159\,s. Taking each page navigation at the real pages' median time to first byte or to the load event (0.67 or 5.49\,s), and each post, download and API call at 0.1 or 0.67\,s, websites would take 643 or 958\,s against 730\,s.
\item \textbf{A cold cache.} After a real wait of hours to days the runtime's 9--15k-token fixed prefix would have left the prompt cache. Writing it again at the one-hour cache-write price would add about \$0.04--0.06 to each session that starts cold: little for the model-run set-ups, and several times the code-run set-ups' whole cost.
\end{itemize}
